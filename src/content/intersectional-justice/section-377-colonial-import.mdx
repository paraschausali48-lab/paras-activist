## The rhetorical inversion

The argument that trans rights are a "Western import" is widespread in Indian public discourse. It is also **historically backwards**.

What was actually imported from the West was **Section 377 of the Indian Penal Code** - drafted by Thomas Macaulay in 1837 and enacted under British colonial rule in **1861**.

Before this, India had no codified law criminalising gender non-conformity or same-sex behaviour.

## The documented Indian history of gender diversity

Hijra, Aravani, Kinnar, and Jogappa communities have documented histories in India spanning **thousands of years**.

- The *Kama Sutra* (Vatsyayana, c. 400 CE) describes gender non-conforming individuals without pathologising language.
- The *Ramayana* contains explicit references to the blessing given by Ram to the "third gender" community - a passage cited in the 2014 NALSA Supreme Court judgment.
- Hijra communities held documented positions in the Mughal imperial court, serving as administrators, advisors, and guardians of the harem - roles requiring significant trust and political standing.
- Multiple regional kingdoms across the Deccan, Rajputana, and Bengal maintained formal relationships with gender non-conforming communities through to the 19th century.

The British colonial project systematically suppressed this history while simultaneously legislating against gender diversity. The Section 377 prosecution record shows a sharp increase in arrests in the late colonial period as enforcement intensified.

## What the Supreme Court said

In the landmark **NALSA v. Union of India (2014)** judgment, the Supreme Court of India:

1. Affirmed that **gender identity is protected** under Articles 14, 15, 16, and 21 of the Constitution.
2. Directed all state governments to implement welfare measures for transgender persons.
3. Explicitly cited the Ramayana and historical records of the Hijra community to establish that gender diversity is **indigenous to India**.

The Court's language was direct: "Recognition of Transgenders as a third gender is not a social or medical issue but a human rights issue."

In **Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India (2018)**, the Supreme Court partially struck down Section 377, decriminalising consensual same-sex relations between adults - describing the colonial law as "irrational, indefensible, and manifestly arbitrary."
