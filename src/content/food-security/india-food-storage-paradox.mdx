India's Food Corporation of India (FCI) is mandated to maintain a specific **buffer stock** of grain - the minimum needed to ensure national food security and price stability. In 2022, the actual stock held was nearly **three times** this norm.

At the same time, the Global Hunger Index 2023 ranked India **111th out of 125 countries**, categorising its hunger situation as **"serious."**

This is not a coincidence. It is the logical outcome of a system designed to procure and store grain - but not to ensure it reaches those who are hungry.

## Why the grain doesn't move

Three structural factors explain the paradox:

**1. The cold-chain gap.** India has roughly 37 million tonnes of cold storage capacity, but a significant portion is dedicated to potato and horticulture, not grain distribution to the poor. Surplus grain rots in FCI warehouses while people go hungry.

**2. The NFSA coverage floor.** The National Food Security Act 2013 covers approximately 67% of India's population. But the population below the poverty line has changed since the last Census (2011). Millions of newly poor or transient-poor households - particularly migrant workers - fall through the gap.

**3. The leakage problem.** Government estimates suggest 30-40% of PDS grain never reaches its intended beneficiaries due to diversion, fake beneficiaries, and logistical failures. The FCI holds the grain; the PDS is meant to distribute it; but between the two, a massive amount disappears.

## What the evidence demands

The evidence does not demand more grain production. It demands:

- A **real-time beneficiary list** tied to current Census data, updated every five years
- Investment in **last-mile cold-chain infrastructure** for perishable foods
- **Community-based monitoring** of PDS distribution at the gram panchayat level
- **Legal accountability** for district-level officials when FCI stocks exceed norms while local hunger indicators remain critical

These are not radical demands. They are engineering and administrative problems with documented solutions.
